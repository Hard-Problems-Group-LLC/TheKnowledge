Development Antipatterns
========================

Scope
-----
This file lists broad software-development antipatterns that routinely reduce
delivery quality, traceability, and recoverability. It stays intentionally
general and avoids language-specific prescriptions unless they reflect a
cross-cutting engineering failure.

Avoid
-----
- Starting implementation before requirements are captured in a specification,
  proposal, or explicit operator direction. Hidden scope turns review and
  testing into guesswork.
- Depending on tribal knowledge, manual machine setup, or a specific
  developer's shell history to build, test, deploy, or recover the system.
- Mixing unrelated behavior changes, refactors, formatting churn, and tooling
  migration in one large change set. Review quality collapses when the unit of
  change stops being coherent.
- Treating testing as a cleanup phase after implementation. Happy-path-only
  validation leaves regressions and operational failure modes undiscovered.
- Letting important state live outside the repository without an authoritative
  record. If a decision, workflow, or recovery step matters, document it where
  the team can version and review it.
- Shipping automation, generated output, or AI-produced changes without human
  verification against the actual specification and runtime behavior.
- Coupling deployment, rollback, or backup strategy to one host or one
  operator. Recovery should be designed, rehearsed, and documented before an
  incident forces it.
- Allowing documentation, issue state, or release notes to drift away from the
  behavior that actually shipped.

Repository recommendation
-------------------------
- Keep changes small enough to review with confidence.
- Record requirements, decisions, and operational steps in versioned files.
- Make build, test, and recovery paths repeatable on a clean machine.
- Validate failure modes and edge cases before calling work complete.
- Treat documentation and state tracking as part of delivery, not post-hoc
  cleanup.
